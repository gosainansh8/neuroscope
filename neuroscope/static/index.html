            Frequently asked

            Questions, answered.

            Where does the information come from?

            Trials from ClinicalTrials.gov, US approvals and labels from the FDA, EU approvals from the European Medicines Agency, abstracts from PubMed, company press releases and annual reports from SEC filings, and mechanisms from ChEMBL, for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and ALS. Everything refreshes every Monday.

            How does it avoid making things up?

            The model never sees the database: it can only request read-only lookups and must answer from what they return. Code, not the model, then checks that every trial, FDA, EMA, SEC, and PubMed ID in the answer came from one of those lookups. The check confirms a source is real, not that it supports the exact sentence, so follow the links for anything important.

            Does "FDA-approved" mean approved for this disease?

            Not necessarily. It means approved for at least one use; semaglutide is approved for diabetes, not Alzheimer's. Approval for a disease comes only from the label or EU indication text, which answers quote. No matched record is not proof a drug is unapproved, and approval is never inferred from a drug reaching Phase 3.

            What is the difference between Answer and Report?

            Answer replies to one question. Report writes a sectioned briefing on a topic (a drug, a pipeline, a comparison) with the same citation check; it makes more lookups, so it counts as three of the 50 daily questions.

            What is the agent team?

            An alternative to the single agent, built with Google's Agent Development Kit: a coordinator asks three specialists (regulatory, clinical, evidence) for what it needs, writes a draft, and a critic checks every sentence against the records the specialists found before the answer is shown. It makes more model calls, so it is slower and counts as three questions; the single agent stays the default. The line under each answer shows the model calls, time, and estimated cost.

            What does "program discontinued" mean?

            It is read from the trial registry: a drug has no active trial left in that disease, and its most recently updated trial was terminated, withdrawn, or suspended. The sponsor's stated reason is quoted when it gives one. It is not a company announcement: a company can end a program without stopping a trial, or continue it in a trial not yet registered.

            Is this medical advice?

            No. It summarizes public research, regulatory, and company records and does not recommend treatments. Talk to a doctor about medical decisions. The code is on GitHub.
